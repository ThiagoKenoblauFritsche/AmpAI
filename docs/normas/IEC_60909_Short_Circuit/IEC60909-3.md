# SHORT-CIRCUIT CURRENTS IN THREE-PHASE AC SYSTEMS -

## Part 3: Currents during two separate simultaneous line-to-earth short circuits and partial short-circuit currents flowing through earth

# 1 Scope and object

This part of IEC 60909 specifies procedures for calculation of the prospective short-circuit currents with an unbalanced short circuit in high-voltage three-phase a.c. systems operating at nominal frequency 50 Hz or 60 Hz, i.e.:

a) currents during two separate simultaneous line-to-earth short circuits in isolated neutral or resonant earthed neutral systems;

b) partial short-circuit currents flowing through earth in case of single line-to-earth short circuit in solidly earthed or low-impedance earthed neutral systems.

The currents calculated by these procedures are used when determining induced voltages or touch or step voltages and rise of earth potential at a station (power station or substation) and the towers of overhead lines.

Procedures are given for the calculation of reduction factors of overhead lines with one or two earth wires.

The standard does not cover:

a) short-circuit currents deliberately created under controlled conditions as in short circuit testing stations, or

b) short-circuit currents in the electrical installations on board ships or aeroplanes, or

c) single line-to-earth fault currents in isolated or resonant earthed systems.

The object of this standard is to establish practical and concise procedures for the calculation of line-to-earth short-circuit currents during two separate simultaneous line-to-earth short circuits and partial short-circuit currents through earth, earth wires of overhead lines and sheaths or shields of cables leading to conservative results with sufficient accuracy. For this purpose, the short-circuit currents are determined by considering an equivalent voltage source at the short-circuit location with all other voltage sources set to zero. Resistances of earth grids in stations or footing resistances of overhead line towers are neglected, when calculating the short-circuit currents at the short-circuit location.

This standard is an addition to IEC 60909-0. General definitions, symbols and calculation assumptions refer to that publication. Special items only are defined or specified in this standard.

The calculation of the short-circuit currents based on the rated data of the electrical equipment and the topological arrangement of the system has the advantage of being possible both for existing systems and for systems at the planning stage. The procedure is suitable for determination by manual methods or digital computation. This does not exclude the use of special methods, for example the super-position method, adjusted to particular circumstances, if they give at least the same precision.

As stated in IEC 60909-0, short-circuit currents and their parameters may also be determined by system tests.

## 3 Terms and definitions

For the purposes of this document, the following terms and definitions apply.

### 3.1

**two separate simultaneous line-to-earth short circuits**

line-to-earth short circuits at different locations at the same time on different conductors of a three-phase a.c. network having a resonant earthed or an isolated neutral

### 3.2

**initial short-circuit currents during two separate simultaneous line-to-earth short circuits** $I''_{\mathrm{kEE}}$

r.m.s value of the initial short-circuit currents flowing at both short-circuit locations with the same magnitude

**partial short-circuit current through earth** $I_{\mathrm{E}\delta}$

r.m.s. value of the current flowing through earth in a fictive line in the equivalent earth penetration depth $\delta$

**total current to earth $I_{\mathrm{ETtot}}$ at the short-circuit location on the tower T of an overhead line**

# 5 Calculation of currents during two separate simultaneous line-to-earth short circuits

## 5.1 Initial symmetrical short-circuit current

Figure 3 shows the short-circuit current $I_{\mathrm{kEE}}^{\prime\prime}$ during two separate simultaneous line-to-earth short circuits on different line conductors at the locations A and B with a finite distance between them. It is assumed that the locations A and B are far from stations.

> **[Figura]**: Schematic diagram (Figure 3) showing three horizontal line conductors L1, L2, and L3 running from left to right. Two fault locations A and B are marked on conductor L1, separated by a distance labelled "Distance > 0". At each fault location, a downward arrow indicates the short-circuit current $I_{\mathrm{kEE}}^{\prime\prime}$ flowing into the earth, represented by a hatched ground symbol at the bottom. The diagram illustrates two separate simultaneous line-to-earth short circuits on different conductors at locations A and B. Reference: IEC 162/09.

NOTE The direction of current arrows is chosen arbitrarily.

**Figure 3 - Characterisation of two separate simultaneous line-to-earth short circuits and the currents $I_{\mathrm{kEE}}^{\prime\prime}$**

In networks with isolated or with resonant earthed neutral the initial symmetrical short-circuit current $I_{\mathrm{kEE}}^{\prime\prime}$ is calculated with

$$
I_{\mathrm{kEE}}^{\prime\prime} = \frac{3c U_{\mathrm{n}}}{\left| \underline{Z}_{(1)\mathrm{A}} + \underline{Z}_{(2)\mathrm{A}} + \underline{Z}_{(1)\mathrm{B}} + \underline{Z}_{(2)\mathrm{B}} + \underline{M}_{(1)} + \underline{M}_{(2)} + \underline{Z}_{(0)} \right|} \tag{4}
$$

NOTE For derivation of Equation (4) see ITU-T - *Directives concerning protection of telecommunication lines against harmful effects from electric power and electrified railway lines, Volume V: Inducing currents and voltages in power transmission and distribution systems*, 1999.

In case of a far-from-generator short circuit, where $\underline{Z}_{(1)} = \underline{Z}_{(2)}$ and $\underline{M}_{(1)} = \underline{M}_{(2)}$, the initial short-circuit current becomes

$$
I_{\mathrm{kEE}}^{\prime\prime} = \frac{3c U_{\mathrm{n}}}{\left| 2\underline{Z}_{(1)\mathrm{A}} + 2\underline{Z}_{(1)\mathrm{B}} + 2\underline{M}_{(1)} + \underline{Z}_{(0)} \right|} \tag{5}
$$

### 5.1.1 Determination of $\underline{M}_{(1)}$ and $\underline{M}_{(2)}$

The positive- and the negative-sequence coupling impedances $\underline{M}_{(1)}$ and $\underline{M}_{(2)}$ are determined as follows:

A voltage source is introduced at the short-circuit location A as the only active voltage of the network. If $\underline{I}_{(1)\mathrm{A}}$ and $\underline{I}_{(2)\mathrm{A}}$ are the currents due to this voltage source in the positive- and the negative-sequence system at the short-circuit location A, and if $\underline{U}_{(1)\mathrm{B}}$ and $\underline{U}_{(2)\mathrm{B}}$ are the resulting voltages in the positive- and negative-sequence system at the location B, then

$$
\underline{M}_{(1)} = \frac{\underline{U}_{(1)\mathrm{B}}}{\underline{I}_{(1)\mathrm{A}}} \qquad \underline{M}_{(2)} = \frac{\underline{U}_{(2)\mathrm{B}}}{\underline{I}_{(2)\mathrm{A}}} \tag{6}
$$

The coupling impedances may also be determined at the short-circuit location B instead of A

$$
\underline{M}_{(1)} = \frac{\underline{U}_{(1)\mathrm{A}}}{\underline{I}_{(1)\mathrm{B}}} \qquad \underline{M}_{(2)} = \frac{\underline{U}_{(2)\mathrm{A}}}{\underline{I}_{(2)\mathrm{B}}} \tag{7}
$$

## 5.2 Peak short-circuit current, symmetrical short circuit breaking current and steady-state short-circuit current

The peak short-circuit current is calculated according to IEC 60909-0:

$$
i_{\mathrm{pEE}} = \kappa \sqrt{2} I''_{\mathrm{kEE}} \tag{11}
$$

For the factor, $\kappa$, the same value is used as in the case of a three-phase short circuit at the locations A or B, whichever is the largest.

If the short circuits can be assumed as far-from-generator short circuits, then

$$
I_{\mathrm{kEE}} = I_{\mathrm{bEE}} = I''_{\mathrm{kEE}} \tag{12}
$$

## 5.3 Distribution of the currents during two separate simultaneous line-to-earth short circuits

If two separate line-to-earth short circuits occur at the locations A and B, the current through earth can be calculated assuming $\underline{I}_{\mathrm{E5}} = \underline{r} \underline{I}''_{\mathrm{kEE}}$, with $\underline{I}''_{\mathrm{kEE}}$ as the only active current source and $\underline{r}$ as the reduction factor of an overhead line with earth wire (Equation 33). For the short circuit at a tower (short-circuit location A or B) far from stations, the current $I_{\mathrm{T}}$ through the footing resistance $R_{\mathrm{T}}$ of the tower is

$$
\underline{I}_{\mathrm{T}} = \underline{r} \underline{I}''_{\mathrm{kEE}} \frac{\underline{Z}_{\mathrm{P}}}{\underline{Z}_{\mathrm{P}} + 2 R_{\mathrm{T}}} \tag{13}
$$

$\underline{Z}_{\mathrm{P}}$ is the driving point impedance of an infinite chain according to Equation (1).

NOTE Equation (13) can be derived from Figure 6 if $\underline{I}_{\mathrm{ETtot}}$ is replaced by $\underline{r} \cdot \underline{I}''_{\mathrm{kEE}}$.

In case of two separate line-to-earth short circuits at overhead lines without earth wire (for instance in medium-voltage networks), the current through earth is equal to the short-circuit current $I''_{\mathrm{kEE}}$.

## 6 Calculation of partial short-circuit currents flowing through earth in case of an unbalanced short circuit

## 6.1 General

The following subclauses deal with partial short-circuit currents flowing through earth and earthed conductors (as earthing systems and earth wires of overhead lines) in the case of a line-to-earth short circuit. This type of short circuit in solidly earthed high-voltage networks is the most frequently occurring unbalanced short circuit. $I''_{\mathrm{k1}}$ leads to the highest short-circuit current to earth compared with the line-to-line short circuit with earth connection if $Z_{(0)} > Z_{(1)}$ (see Figure 10 of IEC 60909-0 in case of $Z_{(2)} = Z_{(1)}$). For $Z_{(0)} < Z_{(1)}$, the current to earth $I''_{\mathrm{kE2E}}$ in case of a line-to-line short circuit with earth connection, shall be considered according to IEC 60909-0.

For the calculation of short-circuit currents according to IEC 60909-0, the tower impedances with or without earth wire and the earth grid impedances and other connections to earth shall be disregarded.

The calculation procedure will be considered on a simplified network consisting of three stations A, B and C, and overhead lines with a single circuit and one earth wire. Moreover, it is assumed that the stations A, B and C are separated by more than twice the far-from-station distance, $D_{\mathrm{F}}$, according to Equation (19).

## 6.2 Line-to-earth short circuit inside a station

Figure 4 shows a transformer station B with feeders coming in from the stations A and C.

Figure 4 - Partial short-circuit currents in case of a line-to-earth short circuit inside station B

The line-to-earth short-circuit current $\underline{I}_{\mathrm{k1}}^{*}$ in Figure 4 is equal to three times the zero-sequence currents flowing to the short-circuit location F:

$$
\underline{I}_{\mathrm{k1}}^{*} = 3\underline{I}_{(0)\mathrm{A}} + 3\underline{I}_{(0)\mathrm{B}} + 3\underline{I}_{(0)\mathrm{C}} \tag{14}
$$

The current $3\underline{I}_{(0)\mathrm{B}}$ is flowing back to the transformer-star point via the earth grid in station B and therefore does not lead to a potential rise at the station B. The currents $3\underline{I}_{(0)\mathrm{A}}$ and $3\underline{I}_{(0)\mathrm{C}}$ are flowing back to the stations A and C through the earth and the earth wires between the station B and the stations A and C. For a far-from-station distance we have (see Figure 4):

$$
3\underline{I}_{(0)\mathrm{A}} = \underline{I}_{\mathrm{E} \delta \mathrm{A}} + \underline{I}_{\mathrm{Q A}} = \underline{r}_{\mathrm{A}} 3\underline{I}_{(0)\mathrm{A}} + (1 - \underline{r}_{\mathrm{A}}) 3\underline{I}_{(0)\mathrm{A}} \tag{15a}
$$

$$
3\underline{I}_{(0)\mathrm{C}} = \underline{I}_{\mathrm{E} \delta \mathrm{C}} + \underline{I}_{\mathrm{Q C}} = \underline{r}_{\mathrm{C}} 3\underline{I}_{(0)\mathrm{C}} + (1 - \underline{r}_{\mathrm{C}}) 3\underline{I}_{(0)\mathrm{C}} \tag{15b}
$$

$\underline{r}_{\mathrm{A}}$ and $\underline{r}_{\mathrm{B}}$ are the reduction factors of the earth wires between B and A and between B and C respectively.

The total current to earth in station B (short-circuit location) is:

$$
\underline{I}_{\mathrm{E B t o t}} = \underline{r}_{\mathrm{A}} 3\underline{I}_{(0)\mathrm{A}} + \underline{r}_{\mathrm{C}} 3\underline{I}_{(0)\mathrm{C}} \tag{16}
$$

The current $\underline{I}_{\mathrm{EBtot}}$ passes the total earthing impedance of station B:

$$
Z_{\mathrm{E B tot}} = \frac{1}{\frac{1}{R_{\mathrm{E B}}} + \sum_{i} \frac{1}{Z_{\mathrm{P} i}} + \sum_{i} \frac{1}{Z_{\mathrm{U} i}}} \tag{17}
$$

where

$R_{\mathrm{EB}}$ is the resistance of the earth grid in station B;

$\underline{Z}_{\mathrm{P}}$ is the driving point impedance according to Equation (1);

$\underline{Z}_{\mathrm{U}}$ is the input impedance of sheaths, shields or armouring of a cables or other metallic pipes or pipelines.

The current through $\underline{Z}_{\mathrm{EBtot}}$ leads to the earth potential $\underline{U}_{\mathrm{EB}}$ at the station B:

$$
\underline {{U}} _ {\mathrm {E B}} = \underline {{Z}} _ {\mathrm {E B t o t}} \underline {{I}} _ {\mathrm {E B t o t}} \tag {18}
$$

The far-from-station distance (far from the short-circuit location) is calculated as follows:

$$
D _ {\mathrm {F}} = 3 \sqrt {R _ {\mathrm {T}}} \frac {d _ {\mathrm {T}}}{\operatorname {R e} \left\{\sqrt {\underline {{Z}} _ {\mathrm {Q}}} \right\}} \tag {19}
$$

where

$R_{\mathrm{T}}$ is the tower footing resistance;

$d_{\mathrm{T}}$ is the distance between towers;

$\mathrm{Re}\left\{\sqrt{\underline{Z}_{\mathrm{Q}}}\right\}$ is the real part of the square root of the earth wire impedance $\underline{Z}_{\mathrm{Q}} = \underline{Z}_{\mathrm{Q}}^{\prime} d_{\mathrm{T}}$ with $\underline{Z}_{\mathrm{Q}}^{\prime}$ from Equation (34).

NOTE 1 If the stations A or C are nearer than $D_{\mathrm{F}}$ to station B, the total current $I_{\mathrm{EBtot}}$ is reduced by an additional part of the currents $\underline{r}_{\mathrm{A}}3\underline{I}_{(0)\mathrm{A}}$ or $\underline{r}_{\mathrm{C}}3\underline{I}_{(0)\mathrm{C}}$ flowing back to the nearest station A or C via earth wires.

NOTE 2 Special considerations may be necessary in the case of double-circuit lines or parallel lines with coupled zero-sequence system.

## 6.3 Line-to-earth short circuit outside a station

A line-to-earth short circuit at a tower of an overhead line is shown in Figure 5. The short circuit is assumed to occur remote from the stations.

Figure 5 - Partial short-circuit currents in case of a line-to-earth short circuit at a tower T of an overhead line

The line-to-earth short-circuit current $I_{\mathrm{k1}}^{*}$ in Figure 5 is equal to three times the zero-sequence currents flowing to the short-circuit location F:

$$
I _ {\mathrm {k} 1} ^ {\prime \prime} = 3 I _ {(0) \mathrm {A}} + 3 I _ {(0) \mathrm {B}} + 3 I _ {(0) \mathrm {C}} \tag {20}
$$

The three currents $3I_{(0)\mathrm{A}}$ , $3I_{(0)\mathrm{B}}$ and $3I_{(0)\mathrm{C}}$ in Figure 5 are flowing back to the stations A, B and C through the earth and the earth wires of the overhead lines between the stations:

$$
3 I _ {(0) \mathrm {A}} + 3 I _ {(0) \mathrm {B}} = I _ {\mathrm {E} \delta \mathrm {A}} + I _ {\mathrm {E} \delta \mathrm {B}} + I _ {\mathrm {Q A}} + I _ {\mathrm {Q B}} = r _ {\mathrm {C}} \left(3 I _ {(0) \mathrm {A}} + 3 I _ {(0) \mathrm {B}}\right) + \left(1 - r _ {\mathrm {C}}\right) \left(3 I _ {(0) \mathrm {A}} + 3 I _ {(0) \mathrm {B}}\right) \tag {21}
$$

and as already known from Figure 4 and the Equations (15):

$$
3 I _ {(0) \mathrm {A}} = I _ {\mathrm {E} \delta \mathrm {A}} + I _ {\mathrm {Q A}} = r _ {\mathrm {A}} 3 I _ {(0) \mathrm {A}} + (1 - r _ {\mathrm {A}}) 3 I _ {(0) \mathrm {A}}
$$

$$
3 I _ {(0) C} = I _ {E \delta C} + I _ {Q C} = r _ {C} 3 I _ {(0) C} + (1 - r _ {C}) 3 I _ {(0) C}
$$

$r_{\mathrm{A}}$ and $r_{\mathrm{B}}$ are the reduction factors of the earth wires between B and A and between B and C respectively.

The total current to earth at the tower T (short-circuit location), far away from stations B and C (distance higher than $D_{\mathrm{F}}$ ) is:

$$
I _ {\mathrm {E T t o t}} = I _ {\mathrm {E} \delta \mathrm {A}} + I _ {\mathrm {E} \delta \mathrm {B}} + I _ {\mathrm {E} \delta \mathrm {C}} = r _ {\mathrm {C}} \left(3 I _ {(0) \mathrm {A}} + 3 I _ {(0) \mathrm {B}} + 3 I _ {(0) \mathrm {C}}\right) = r _ {\mathrm {C}} I _ {\mathrm {k} 1} ^ {*} \tag {22}
$$

This current passes the total earthing impedance of the short-circuited tower $T$ connected to the earth wire of the overhead line $\overline{BC}$ according to Figure 6:

$$
Z _ {\mathrm {E T t o t}} = \frac {1}{\frac {1}{R _ {\mathrm {T}}} + \frac {2}{Z _ {\mathrm {P}}}} \tag {23}
$$

$R_{\mathrm{T}}$ is the footing resistance of the tower and $Z_{\mathrm{P}}$ the driving point impedance according to Figure 1.

Figure 6 - Distribution of the total current to earth $I_{\mathrm{ETtot}}$

$I_{\mathrm{T}}$ is found from Equation (13), if $r I_{\mathrm{kEE}}^{*}$ is replaced by $r_{\mathrm{C}} I_{\mathrm{k1}}^{*}$ .

The current through $\underline{Z}_{\mathrm{ETtot}}$ leads to the earth potential $\underline{U}_{\mathrm{ET}}$ at the short-circuited tower (see Figure 6).

$$
\underline {{U}} _ {\mathrm {E T}} = \underline {{Z}} _ {\mathrm {E T t o t}} \underline {{I}} _ {\mathrm {E T t o t}} = R _ {\mathrm {T}} \underline {{I}} _ {\mathrm {T}} \tag {24}
$$

If the line-to-earth short circuit occurs on a tower in the vicinity of station B, then the earth potential may be higher than the result found with Equation (24). A determination needs special consideration as given in 6.4.

The current to earth in station B in the case of a line-to-earth short circuit at the tower T (distance higher than $D_{\mathrm{F}}$ from station B) is according to Figure 5 found from:

$$
\underline {{I}} _ {\mathrm {E B t o t}} = \underline {{r}} _ {\mathrm {C}} \left(3 \underline {{I}} _ {(0) \mathrm {A}} + 3 \underline {{I}} _ {(0) \mathrm {B}}\right) - \underline {{r}} _ {\mathrm {A}} 3 \underline {{I}} _ {(0) \mathrm {A}} \tag {25}
$$

The earth potential of station B with the current $\underline{I}_{\mathrm{EBtot}}$ from Equation (25) becomes in this case:

$$
\underline {{U}} _ {\mathrm {E B}} = \underline {{Z}} _ {\mathrm {E B t o t}} \underline {{I}} _ {\mathrm {E B t o t}} \tag {26}
$$

If the line-to-earth short circuit occurs on a tower in the vicinity of station B, then the current to earth in station B may be higher than $\underline{I}_{\mathrm{EBtot}}$ found from Equation (16) in case of a line-to-earth short circuit in station B (Figure 4). A determination needs special conditions as given in 6.4.

## 6.4 Line-to-earth short circuit in the vicinity of a station

If the line-to earth short circuit occurs at a tower in the vicinity (distance smaller than $D_{\mathrm{F}}$ ) of a station (Figure 7), then the earth potential $\underline{U}_{\mathrm{EBn}}$ (additional index $n$ to point out that the line-to-earth short circuit occurs at a tower $n = 0, 1, 2, 3 \ldots$ outside the station) may be higher than $\underline{U}_{\mathrm{EB}}$ calculated with Equation (18). In this case, the earth potential $\underline{U}_{\mathrm{ETn}}$ of the short-circuited tower $n$ in the vicinity of station B is also higher than the earth potential $\underline{U}_{\mathrm{ET}}$ for a tower far outside station B calculated with Equation (24).

Figure 7 - Partial short-circuit currents in the case of a line-to-earth short circuit at a tower $n$ of an overhead line in the vicinity of station B

Following Figure 2, the numbering of the towers has to be taken into account, when calculating $\underline{I}''_{\mathrm{k1}}$ and $3\underline{I}_{(0)\mathrm{B}}$.

## 6.4.1 Earth potential $\underline{U}_{\mathrm{ET}n}$ at the tower $n$ outside station B

The current $\underline{I}_{\mathrm{ET}n}$ (Figure 7) at the short-circuited tower in the vicinity of station B flowing through $\underline{Z}_{\mathrm{ET}}$ according to Equation (28) is found from the superposition of two terms, the first one depending on the current to earth $\underline{r}_{\mathrm{C}}\underline{I}''_{\mathrm{k1}}$ at the short-circuit location and the second one depending on the current $\underline{r}_{\mathrm{C}}3\underline{I}_{(0)\mathrm{B}}$ flowing back through earth to the star point of the transformer in station B.

$$
\underline{I}_{\mathrm{ET}n} = \underline{r}_{\mathrm{C}}\underline{I}''_{\mathrm{k1}} \frac{\underline{Z}_{\mathrm{P}n}}{\underline{Z}_{\mathrm{P}n} + \underline{Z}_{\mathrm{ET}}} - \underline{r}_{\mathrm{C}}3\underline{I}_{(0)\mathrm{B}} \frac{\underline{Z}_{\mathrm{EB}}}{\underline{Z}_{\mathrm{EB}} + \underline{Z}_{\mathrm{P}}} \cdot \frac{1}{\underline{k}^{n}} \tag{27}
$$

with

$$
\underline{Z}_{\mathrm{ET}} = \frac{1}{\dfrac{1}{R_{\mathrm{T}}} + \dfrac{1}{\underline{Z}_{\mathrm{P}}}} \tag{28}
$$

and

$$
\underline{Z}_{\mathrm{EB}} = \frac{1}{\dfrac{1}{R_{\mathrm{EB}}} + \dfrac{1}{\underline{Z}_{\mathrm{P}}}} \tag{29}
$$

The driving point Impedance $\underline{Z}_{\mathrm{P}}$ is found from Equation (1), $\underline{Z}_{\mathrm{P}n}$ from Equation (2) and $\underline{k}$ from Equation (3).

The earth potential $\underline{U}_{\mathrm{ET}n}$ at the short-circuited tower $n$ is found with:

$$
\underline{U}_{\mathrm{ET}n} = \underline{Z}_{\mathrm{ET}}\underline{I}_{\mathrm{ET}n} \tag{30}
$$

## 6.4.2 Earth potential of station B during a line-to-earth short circuit at the tower $n$

The current $\underline{I}_{\mathrm{EB}n}$ passing $\underline{Z}_{\mathrm{EB}}$ in Figure 7 in case of a line-to-earth short circuit at a tower $n$ in the vicinity of station B is found with the following equation:

$$
\underline{I}_{\mathrm{EB}n} = \underline{r}_{\mathrm{C}}\underline{I}''_{\mathrm{k1}} \frac{\underline{Z}_{\mathrm{ET}}}{\underline{Z}_{\mathrm{ET}} + \underline{Z}_{\mathrm{P}n}} \cdot \frac{2\underline{Z}_{\mathrm{P}} - \underline{Z}_{\mathrm{Q}}}{(\underline{Z}_{\mathrm{EB}} + \underline{Z}_{\mathrm{P}})\underline{k}^{n} - (\underline{Z}_{\mathrm{EB}} - \underline{Z}_{\mathrm{P}} + \underline{Z}_{\mathrm{Q}})\underline{k}^{-n}} - \underline{r}_{\mathrm{C}}3\underline{I}_{(0)\mathrm{B}} \frac{\underline{Z}_{\mathrm{P}}}{\underline{Z}_{\mathrm{EB}} + \underline{Z}_{\mathrm{P}}} \tag{31}
$$

The earth potential of station B during the line-to-earth short circuit at the tower $n$ in the vicinity of station B is:

$$
\underline{U}_{\mathrm{EB}n} = \underline{Z}_{\mathrm{EB}}\underline{I}_{\mathrm{EB}n} \tag{32}
$$

# 7 Reduction factor for overhead lines with earth wires

The reduction factor of overhead lines with earth wires can be calculated as follows:

$$
\underline{r} = \frac{I_{E\delta}}{3I_{(0)}} = 1 - \frac{\underline{Z}_{\mathrm{QL}}'}{\underline{Z}_{\mathrm{Q}}'} \tag{33}
$$

$\underline{Z}_{\mathrm{Q}}'$ and $\underline{Z}_{\mathrm{QL}}'$ shall be calculated with the Equations (34) and (35). The result depends on the soil resistivity $\rho$, the distance $d_{\mathrm{QL}}$ between the earth wire and the line conductors and the equivalent earth wire radius, $r_{\mathrm{QQ}}$, for one or more earth wires.

Table 2 - Resistivity of the soil and equivalent earth penetration depth

| Soil types | Soil resistivity $\rho$ Ωm | Equivalent earth penetration depth $\delta$ m | |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| | | for 50 Hz | for 60 Hz |
| Granite | >10 000 | >9 300 | >8 500 |
| Rocks | 3 000 ... 10 000 | 5 100 ... 9 330 | 4 670 ... 8 520 |
| Stony soil | 1 000 ... 3 000 | 2 950 ... 5 110 | 2 690 ... 4 670 |
| Pebbles, dry sand | 200 ... 1 200 | 1 320 ... 3 230 | 1 200 ... 2 950 |

| Marshy soil | <20 | <420 | <380 |

The earth wire impedance per unit length with earth return is

$$
\underline{Z}_{\mathrm{Q}}' = \frac{R_{\mathrm{Q}}'}{\nu} + \omega \frac{\mu_0}{8} + \mathrm{j} \omega \frac{\mu_0}{2\pi} \left( \frac{\mu_r}{4\nu} + \ln \frac{\delta}{r_{\mathrm{QQ}}} \right) \tag{34}
$$

and the mutual impedance per unit length between the earth wire and the line conductors with earth return

$$
\underline{Z}_{\mathrm{QL}}' = \omega \frac{\mu_0}{8} + \mathrm{j} \omega \frac{\mu_0}{2\pi} \ln \frac{\delta}{d_{\mathrm{QL}}} \tag{35}
$$

The equivalent earth penetration depth $\delta$ depends on the soil type as given in Table 2 and can be found as follows:

$$
\delta = \frac{1,851}{\sqrt{\omega \frac{\mu_0}{\rho}}} \tag{36}
$$

The following definitions apply:

- $R_{\mathrm{Q}}'$ Earth wire resistance per unit length
- $d_{\mathrm{Q1Q2}}$ Distance between the two earth wires Q1 and Q2

$r_{\mathrm{Q}}$ &emsp; Earth wire radius

$r_{\mathrm{QQ}}$ &emsp; Equivalent earth wire radius

&emsp;&emsp; for one earth wire: $r_{\mathrm{QQ}} = r_{\mathrm{Q}}$

&emsp;&emsp; for two earth wires: $r_{\mathrm{QQ}} = \sqrt{r_{\mathrm{Q}}\,d_{\mathrm{Q1Q2}}}$

$\nu$ &emsp; Number of earth wires $(\nu = 1, 2)$

$d_{\mathrm{QL}}$ &emsp; Mean geometric distance between the earth wire and the line conductors

&emsp;&emsp; for one earth wire: $d_{\mathrm{QL}} = \sqrt[3]{d_{\mathrm{QL1}}\,d_{\mathrm{QL2}}\,d_{\mathrm{QL3}}}$

&emsp;&emsp; for two earth wires: $d_{\mathrm{QL}} = \sqrt[6]{d_{\mathrm{Q1L1}}\,d_{\mathrm{Q1L2}}\,d_{\mathrm{Q1L3}}\,d_{\mathrm{Q2L1}}\,d_{\mathrm{Q2L2}}\,d_{\mathrm{Q2L3}}}$

$\mu_{\mathrm{r}}$ &emsp; Relative permeability of the earth wire material

&emsp;&emsp; Aluminium core steel reinforced (ACSR) wires with one layer of aluminium: $\mu_{\mathrm{r}} = 5 \ldots 10$;

&emsp;&emsp; Other ACSR wires: $\mu_{\mathrm{r}} \approx 1$;

&emsp;&emsp; Steel wires: $\mu_{\mathrm{r}} \approx 75$.

According to Equation (34) and (35), the reduction factor of usual ACSR earth wires depends on the soil resistivity $\rho$. Figure 8 shows reduction factors for non-magnetic earth wires of different overhead lines with nominal voltages 60 kV to 220 kV.

## 8 Calculation of current distribution and reduction factor in case of cables with metallic sheath or shield earthed at both ends

### 8.1 Overview

The reduction factor of power cables with metallic sheath, shield and armouring earthed at both ends depends on the type of cable: Three-core cable with a common sheath, three single-core cables with three sheaths or shields and, in some cases, with additional armouring, the cross-section of the metallic sheath(s) or shield(s) in compliance with national techniques and standards.

Reduction factor of cables with steel armouring shall be given from the manufacturer (see IEC/TR 60909-2).

It is anticipated in this standard that the cables have an outer thermoplastic sheath (see IEC/TR 60909-2).

## 8.2 Three-core cable

Figure 9 gives the configurations dealt with in case of a three-core cable with metallic sheath or shield earthed at both ends and an outer thermoplastic sheath isolating the cable against the surrounding soil.

## 8.2.1 Line-to-earth short circuit in station B

In the case of Figure 9a), if the cable is fed from side A only with a line-to-earth short-circuit current $\underline{I}_{\mathrm{k1}}^{\prime} = 3\underline{I}_{(0)\mathrm{A}}$ in station B, the reduction factor $\underline{r}_1$ gives that part $\underline{I}_{\mathrm{E}\delta\mathrm{A}} = \underline{r}_1 3\underline{I}_{(0)\mathrm{A}}$ of the line-to-earth short-circuit current, that is flowing back through earth by the induction effect.

$$
\underline{r}_{1} = \frac{\underline{I}_{\mathrm{E}\delta\mathrm{A}}}{3\underline{I}_{(0)\mathrm{A}}} = 1 - \frac{\underline{Z}_{\mathrm{SL}}^{\prime}}{\underline{Z}_{\mathrm{S}}^{\prime}} = \frac{R_{\mathrm{S}}^{\prime}}{R_{\mathrm{S}}^{\prime} + \omega\dfrac{\mu_{0}}{8} + \mathrm{j}\omega\dfrac{\mu_{0}}{2\pi}\ln\dfrac{\delta}{r_{\mathrm{S}}}} \tag{37}
$$

$\underline{Z}_{\mathrm{S}}^{\prime}$ is the impedance of the metallic (non magnetic) sheath (or shield) per unit length with earth return:

$$
\underline{Z}_{\mathrm{S}}^{\prime} = R_{\mathrm{S}}^{\prime} + \omega\frac{\mu_{0}}{8} + \mathrm{j}\omega\frac{\mu_{0}}{2\pi}\ln\frac{\delta}{r_{\mathrm{S}}} \tag{38}
$$

$\underline{Z}_{\mathrm{SL}}^{\prime}$ is the mutual impedance between the sheath and one of the cores (inside the sheath, independently of the position) per unit length with earth return:

$$
\underline{Z}_{\mathrm{SL}}^{\prime} = \omega\frac{\mu_{0}}{8} + \mathrm{j}\omega\frac{\mu_{0}}{2\pi}\ln\frac{\delta}{r_{\mathrm{S}}} \tag{39}
$$

where

$R_{\mathrm{S}}^{\prime}$ &emsp; Resistance per unit length of the sheath or shield (copper, aluminium, lead); $R_{\mathrm{S}}^{\prime} = 1/(\kappa \cdot q_{\mathrm{S}})$ with $q_{\mathrm{S}} \approx 2\pi r_{\mathrm{S}} d_{\mathrm{S}}$ where $d_{\mathrm{S}}$ is the thickness of the sheath or shield

$r_{\mathrm{S}}$ &emsp; Medium radius of the sheath or shield;

$\delta$ &emsp; Equivalent earth penetration depth (Table 2 or Equation (36)).

The current in the sheath or shield in Figure 9a) is calculated as follows:

$$
\underline{I}_{\mathrm{SA}} = \left(1 - \underline{r}_{1}\right) 3\underline{I}_{(0)\mathrm{A}} \tag{40}
$$

The current through earth in Figure 9a) is found with:

$$
\underline{I}_{\mathrm{E\delta A}} = \underline{r}_{1} 3\underline{I}_{(0)\mathrm{A}} \tag{41}
$$

## 8.2.2 Line-to-earth short circuit on the cable between station A and station B

In case of a line-to-earth short circuit on the cable between the stations A and B the currents in the sheath or shield in Figure 9b) are calculated as follows:

$$
\underline{I}_{\mathrm{SA}} = (1 - \underline{r}_{1}) 3\underline{I}_{(0)\mathrm{A}} + \underline{r}_{1} 3\underline{I}_{(0)\mathrm{A}} \frac{\underline{Z}_{\mathrm{EStot}}}{\underline{Z}_{\mathrm{S}}^{\ell}{}_{\mathrm{A}}} + \underline{r}_{1} 3\underline{I}_{(0)\mathrm{B}} \frac{\underline{Z}_{\mathrm{EStot}}}{\underline{Z}_{\mathrm{S}}^{\ell}{}_{\mathrm{A}}} \tag{42}
$$

$$
\underline{I}_{\mathrm{SB}} = (1 - \underline{r}_{1}) 3\underline{I}_{(0)\mathrm{B}} + \underline{r}_{1} 3\underline{I}_{(0)\mathrm{B}} \frac{\underline{Z}_{\mathrm{EStot}}}{\underline{Z}_{\mathrm{S}}^{\ell}{}_{\mathrm{B}}} + \underline{r}_{1} 3\underline{I}_{(0)\mathrm{A}} \frac{\underline{Z}_{\mathrm{EStot}}}{\underline{Z}_{\mathrm{S}}^{\ell}{}_{\mathrm{B}}} \tag{43}
$$

The current to earth at the short-circuit location is given as:

$$
\underline{I}_{\mathrm{EF}} = \underline{r}_{1} 3\underline{I}_{(0)\mathrm{A}} \frac{\underline{Z}_{\mathrm{EStot}}}{R_{\mathrm{EF}}} + \underline{r}_{1} 3\underline{I}_{(0)\mathrm{B}} \frac{\underline{Z}_{\mathrm{EStot}}}{R_{\mathrm{EF}}} \tag{44}
$$

The currents through earth in Figure 9b) are found with:

$$
\underline{I}_{\mathrm{E}\delta\mathrm{A}} = \underline{r}_{1}3\underline{I}_{(0)\mathrm{A}}\frac{\underline{Z}_{\mathrm{ESTot}}}{\underline{Z}_{\mathrm{S}}^{\prime}\ell_{\mathrm{B}}} + \underline{r}_{1}3\underline{I}_{(0)\mathrm{A}}\frac{\underline{Z}_{\mathrm{ESTot}}}{R_{\mathrm{EF}}} - \underline{r}_{1}3\underline{I}_{(0)\mathrm{B}}\frac{\underline{Z}_{\mathrm{ESTot}}}{\underline{Z}_{\mathrm{S}}^{\prime}\ell_{\mathrm{A}}} \tag{45}
$$

$$
\underline{I}_{\mathrm{E}\delta\mathrm{B}} = \underline{r}_{1}3\underline{I}_{(0)\mathrm{B}}\frac{\underline{Z}_{\mathrm{ESTot}}}{\underline{Z}_{\mathrm{S}}^{\prime}\ell_{\mathrm{A}}} + \underline{r}_{1}3\underline{I}_{(0)\mathrm{B}}\frac{\underline{Z}_{\mathrm{ESTot}}}{R_{\mathrm{EF}}} - \underline{r}_{1}3\underline{I}_{(0)\mathrm{A}}\frac{\underline{Z}_{\mathrm{ESTot}}}{\underline{Z}_{\mathrm{S}}^{\prime}\ell_{\mathrm{B}}} \tag{46}
$$

with

$$
\underline{Z}_{\mathrm{ESTot}} = \frac{1}{\dfrac{1}{\underline{Z}_{\mathrm{S}}^{\prime}\ell_{\mathrm{A}}} + \dfrac{1}{\underline{Z}_{\mathrm{S}}^{\prime}\ell_{\mathrm{B}}} + \dfrac{1}{R_{\mathrm{EF}}}} = \frac{\underline{Z}_{\mathrm{S}}^{\prime}\ell_{\mathrm{A}}\ell_{\mathrm{B}}}{\ell + \dfrac{\underline{Z}_{\mathrm{S}}^{\prime}\ell_{\mathrm{A}}\ell_{\mathrm{B}}}{R_{\mathrm{EF}}}} \tag{47}
$$

The reduction factor $\underline{r}_{1}$ is given in Equation (37).

The given equations are valid for a cable length of at least $\ell \approx \delta/2$ ($\ell \approx 0{,}5\,\mathrm{km}$ in case of $\rho = 100\,\Omega\mathrm{m}$) between the stations A and B in Figure 9a), and distances $\ell_{\mathrm{A}}$ and $\ell_{\mathrm{B}}$ between the short-circuit location and the adjacent stations A and B in Figure 9b), for at least $\ell \approx \delta/2$ in Case 2 according to 8.2.2.2.

Because in normal cases the resistance $R_{\mathrm{EF}}$ at the short-circuit location against reference earth is not known, the two cases $R_{\mathrm{EF}} \rightarrow \infty$ (there is no connection at the short-circuit location between the metallic sheath (shield) of the cable and the surrounding soil) and $R_{\mathrm{EF}} \rightarrow \mathrm{Min.}$ have to be taken into account.

## 8.2.2.1 Case 1: $R_{\mathrm{EF}} \rightarrow \infty$

In case of $R_{\mathrm{EF}} \rightarrow \infty$, it is anticipated that the outer thermoplastic sheath is not destroyed by the short-circuit current or by the arc at the short-circuit location. The following expressions are found from the Equations (42) and (43):

$$
\underline{I}_{\mathrm{SA}} = (1 - \underline{r}_{1})3\underline{I}_{(0)\mathrm{A}} + \underline{r}_{1}3\underline{I}_{(0)\mathrm{A}}\frac{\ell_{\mathrm{B}}}{\ell} + \underline{r}_{1}3\underline{I}_{(0)\mathrm{B}}\frac{\ell_{\mathrm{B}}}{\ell} \tag{42a}
$$

$$
\underline{I}_{\mathrm{SB}} = (1 - \underline{r}_{1})3\underline{I}_{(0)\mathrm{B}} + \underline{r}_{1}3\underline{I}_{(0)\mathrm{B}}\frac{\ell_{\mathrm{A}}}{\ell} + \underline{r}_{1}3\underline{I}_{(0)\mathrm{A}}\frac{\ell_{\mathrm{A}}}{\ell} \tag{43a}
$$

In this case the Equations (45) and (46) lead to:

$$
\underline{I}_{\mathrm{E}\delta\mathrm{A}} = \underline{r}_{1}3\underline{I}_{(0)\mathrm{A}}\frac{\ell_{\mathrm{A}}}{\ell} - \underline{r}_{1}3\underline{I}_{(0)\mathrm{B}}\frac{\ell_{\mathrm{B}}}{\ell} \tag{45a}
$$

$$
\underline{I}_{\mathrm{E}\delta\mathrm{B}} = \underline{r}_{1}3\underline{I}_{(0)\mathrm{B}}\frac{\ell_{\mathrm{B}}}{\ell} - \underline{r}_{1}3\underline{I}_{(0)\mathrm{A}}\frac{\ell_{\mathrm{A}}}{\ell} \tag{46a}
$$

The line-to-earth short-circuit current at the short-circuit location between A and B shall be calculated with the zero-sequence impedance per unit length $\underline{Z}_{(0)\mathrm{S}}^{\prime}$ of the cable for a current return only through the sheath or shield (see IEC/TR 60909-2 and the information about the calculation of this value given in IEC/TR 60909-2, Equations (30) and (31)).

The highest current through the sheath or shield will occur, if the short-circuit location is near the station A or the station B and if the short circuit in Figure 9b) is fed from both sides.

$$
\underline{I}_{\mathrm{SAmax}} = 3\underline{I}_{(0)\mathrm{A}} (\ell_{\mathrm{A}} = 0) + \underline{r}_{1} 3\underline{I}_{(0)\mathrm{B}} (\ell_{\mathrm{A}} = 0) \tag{42b}
$$

$$
\underline{I}_{\mathrm{SBmax}} = 3\underline{I}_{(0)\mathrm{B}} (\ell_{\mathrm{A}} = \ell) + \underline{r}_{1} 3\underline{I}_{(0)\mathrm{A}} (\ell_{\mathrm{A}} = \ell) \tag{43b}
$$

The highest current through earth is also found if the short circuit occurs near the station A or B:

$$
\underline{I}_{\mathrm{E\delta Amax}} = \underline{r}_{1} 3\underline{I}_{(0)\mathrm{A}} (\ell_{\mathrm{A}} = \ell) \tag{45b}
$$

$$
\underline{I}_{\mathrm{E\delta Bmax}} = \underline{r}_{1} 3\underline{I}_{(0)\mathrm{B}} (\ell_{\mathrm{A}} = 0) \tag{46b}
$$

## 8.2.2.2 Case 2: $R_{\mathrm{EF}} = 5\ \Omega$

The value $R_{\mathrm{EF}} = 5\ \Omega$ is to be seen as a conservative hypothesis, because the area of the connection to the surrounding soil is small even if the thermoplastic outer sheath is destroyed. When fixing this value, it is anticipated, that the short-circuit location is outside the stations A and B and that no metallic rods or pipes are in the neighbourhood of the short-circuit location.

In this case, the line-to-earth short-circuit current $\underline{I}''_{\mathrm{k1}}$ at the short-circuit location between A and B shall be calculated with the zero-sequence impedance $\underline{Z}'_{(0)\mathrm{SE}}$ for a current return through the sheath or shield of the cable and the earth (see IEC/TR 60909-2).

The currents in the sheath or shield and through the earth shall be calculated with Equations (42), (43) and (45), (46).

The highest current through the sheath or the shield can be calculated with Equations (42b) and (43b).

If the highest values for the currents through earth are searched for, use the highest line-to-earth short-circuit current fed from one side of the cable only and neglect the current fed from the other side. In this case Equations (45) and (46) lead to:

$$
\underline{I}_{\mathrm{E\delta Amax}} = \underline{r}_{1} 3\underline{I}_{(0)\mathrm{A}} \left(\frac{Z_{\mathrm{EStot}}}{\underline{Z}'_{\mathrm{S}} \ell_{\mathrm{B}}} + \frac{Z_{\mathrm{EStot}}}{R_{\mathrm{EF}}}\right) \tag{45c}
$$

$$
\underline{I}_{\mathrm{E\delta Bmax}} = \underline{r}_{1} 3\underline{I}_{(0)\mathrm{B}} \left(\frac{Z_{\mathrm{EStot}}}{\underline{Z}'_{\mathrm{S}} \ell_{\mathrm{A}}} + \frac{Z_{\mathrm{EStot}}}{R_{\mathrm{EF}}}\right) \tag{46c}
$$

Calculations with the above equations may lead to higher currents through earth than those found with Equations (45b) or (46b).

NOTE Clause C.2 gives an example for the calculations, if the highest currents through earth are searched for.

If the cable has an additional iron armouring (for instance in the case of a lead sheath), the manufacturer shall give the reduction factor (depending on the current through the sheath) found for instance from measurements. See for information IEC/TR 60909-2.

## 8.3 Three single-core cables

As given under 8.2, in this case also a distinction shall be made between a line-to-earth short circuit in station B, if the short-current is fed from station A (Figure 10a)) or if the short circuit is on the cable at a location between the station A and B (Figure 10b)).

## 8.3.1 Line-to-earth short circuit in station B

In case of three single-core cables in Figure 10a), with three sheaths (shields) earthed and connected at both ends, the reduction factor $\underline{r}_3$ shall be calculated as follows:

$$
\underline{r}_3 = 1 - \frac{\underline{I}_{\mathrm{S1}} + \underline{I}_{\mathrm{S2}} + \underline{I}_{\mathrm{S3}}}{3 \underline{I}_{(0)}} = \frac{R_{\mathrm{S}}'}{R_{\mathrm{S}}' + 3 \cdot \omega \dfrac{\mu_0}{8} + j3 \cdot \omega \dfrac{\mu_0}{2\pi} \ln \dfrac{\delta}{\sqrt[3]{r_{\mathrm{S}} d_{\mathrm{L1L2}} d_{\mathrm{L1L3}}}}} \tag{48}
$$

The distances $d_{\mathrm{L1L2}}$ and $d_{\mathrm{L1L3}}$ according to Figure 10a) shall be used for a triangular and a flat configuration. The result found from Equation (48) is the exact result for a triangular configuration. For a flat configuration the result of Equation (48) can be used as a sufficient approximation for this standard, independently if the line-to-earth short-circuit current will occur in an outer cable or the central cable of the flat configuration.

The sum of the currents through the three sheaths or shields according to Figure 10a) is calculated as follows:

$$
\underline{I}_{\mathrm{SA}} = \underline{I}_{\mathrm{S1A}} + \underline{I}_{\mathrm{S2A}} + \underline{I}_{\mathrm{S3A}} = (1 - \underline{r}_3) 3 \underline{I}_{(0)\mathrm{A}} \tag{49}
$$

The current through earth, flowing back to station A of Figure 10a), is found with the reduction factor $\underline{r}_3$ from Equation (48):

$$
\underline{I}_{\mathrm{E\delta A}} = \underline{r}_3 \, 3 \underline{I}_{(0)\mathrm{A}} \tag{50}
$$

## 8.3.2 Line-to-earth short circuit on the cable between station A and station B

In case of a line-to-earth short circuit on the cable between the stations A and B, fed from both sides in Figure 10b), currents generally are flowing in the three line conductors and in the three sheaths or shields of the single core cables.

The sum of the currents in the three sheaths or shields are calculated as follows:

$$
\underline{I}_{\mathrm{SA}} = (1 - \underline{r}_3) 3 \underline{I}_{(0)\mathrm{A}} + \underline{r}_3 3 \underline{I}_{(0)\mathrm{A}} \frac{\underline{Z}_{\mathrm{EStot}}}{\underline{Z}'_{\mathrm{S}} \ell_{\mathrm{A}}} + \underline{r}_3 3 \underline{I}_{(0)\mathrm{B}} \frac{\underline{Z}_{\mathrm{EStot}}}{\underline{Z}'_{\mathrm{S}} \ell_{\mathrm{A}}} \tag{51}
$$

$$
\underline{I}_{\mathrm{SB}} = (1 - \underline{r}_3) 3 \underline{I}_{(0)\mathrm{B}} + \underline{r}_3 3 \underline{I}_{(0)\mathrm{B}} \frac{\underline{Z}_{\mathrm{EStot}}}{\underline{Z}'_{\mathrm{S}} \ell_{\mathrm{B}}} + \underline{r}_3 3 \underline{I}_{(0)\mathrm{A}} \frac{\underline{Z}_{\mathrm{EStot}}}{\underline{Z}'_{\mathrm{S}} \ell_{\mathrm{B}}} \tag{52}
$$

The current to earth at the short circuit location is given as:

$$
\underline{I}_{\mathrm{EF}} = \underline{r}_3 3\underline{I}_{(0)\mathrm{A}} \frac{Z_{\mathrm{EStot}}}{R_{\mathrm{EF}}} + \underline{r}_3 3\underline{I}_{(0)\mathrm{B}} \frac{Z_{\mathrm{EStot}}}{R_{\mathrm{EF}}} \tag{53}
$$

The currents through earth are found with:

$$
\underline{I}_{\mathrm{E}\delta\mathrm{A}} = \underline{r}_3 3\underline{I}_{(0)\mathrm{A}} \frac{Z_{\mathrm{EStot}}}{\underline{Z}_{\mathrm{S}}^{\prime} \ell_{\mathrm{B}}} + \underline{r}_3 3\underline{I}_{(0)\mathrm{A}} \frac{Z_{\mathrm{EStot}}}{R_{\mathrm{EF}}} - \underline{r}_3 3\underline{I}_{(0)\mathrm{B}} \frac{Z_{\mathrm{EStot}}}{\underline{Z}_{\mathrm{S}}^{\prime} \ell_{\mathrm{A}}} \tag{54}
$$

$$
\underline{I}_{\mathrm{E}\delta\mathrm{B}} = \underline{r}_3 3\underline{I}_{(0)\mathrm{B}} \frac{Z_{\mathrm{EStot}}}{\underline{Z}_{\mathrm{S}}^{\prime} \ell_{\mathrm{A}}} + \underline{r}_3 3\underline{I}_{(0)\mathrm{B}} \frac{Z_{\mathrm{EStot}}}{R_{\mathrm{EF}}} - \underline{r}_3 3\underline{I}_{(0)\mathrm{A}} \frac{Z_{\mathrm{EStot}}}{\underline{Z}_{\mathrm{S}}^{\prime} \ell_{\mathrm{B}}} \tag{55}
$$

with $\underline{Z}_{\text{EStot}}$ according to Equation (47).

In this case $\underline{Z}_{\mathrm{S}}^{\prime}$ is the self impedance per unit length of one of the three sheaths or shields, calculated with Equation (38).

In case of $R_{\mathrm{EF}} \rightarrow \infty$, it is anticipated that the outer thermoplastic sheath is not destroyed by the short-circuit current or by the arc at the short-circuit location. The following expressions are found from Equations (51) and (52):

$$
\underline{I}_{\mathrm{SA}} = (1 - \underline{r}_{3}) 3 \underline{I}_{(0)\mathrm{A}} + \underline{r}_{3} 3 \underline{I}_{(0)\mathrm{A}} \frac{\ell_{\mathrm{B}}}{\ell} + \underline{r}_{3} 3 \underline{I}_{(0)\mathrm{B}} \frac{\ell_{\mathrm{B}}}{\ell} \tag{51a}
$$

$$
\underline{I}_{\mathrm{SB}} = (1 - \underline{r}_{3}) 3 \underline{I}_{(0)\mathrm{B}} + \underline{r}_{3} 3 \underline{I}_{(0)\mathrm{B}} \frac{\ell_{\mathrm{A}}}{\ell} + \underline{r}_{3} 3 \underline{I}_{(0)\mathrm{A}} \frac{\ell_{\mathrm{A}}}{\ell} \tag{52a}
$$

$$
\underline{I}_{\mathrm{E\delta A}} = \underline{r}_{3} 3 \underline{I}_{(0)\mathrm{A}} \frac{\ell_{\mathrm{A}}}{\ell} - \underline{r}_{3} 3 \underline{I}_{(0)\mathrm{B}} \frac{\ell_{\mathrm{B}}}{\ell} \tag{54a}
$$

$$
\underline{I}_{\mathrm{E\delta B}} = \underline{r}_{3} 3 \underline{I}_{(0)\mathrm{B}} \frac{\ell_{\mathrm{B}}}{\ell} - \underline{r}_{3} 3 \underline{I}_{(0)\mathrm{A}} \frac{\ell_{\mathrm{A}}}{\ell} \tag{55a}
$$

$\ell_{\mathrm{A}} = \ell$ $\qquad\qquad\qquad \underline{I}_{\mathrm{S1Bmax}} \approx 3 \underline{I}_{(0)\mathrm{B}} + (2 + \underline{r}_{3}) \underline{I}_{(0)\mathrm{A}} \tag{52b}$

$\ell_{\mathrm{A}} = 0$ $\qquad\qquad\qquad \underline{I}_{\mathrm{E\delta A\,max}} = -\underline{r}_{3} 3 \underline{I}_{(0)\mathrm{B}} \tag{54b}$

The sum of the currents in the sheaths or shields and the currents through earth shall be calculated with Equations (51), (52) and (54), (55).

$$
\underline{I}_{\mathrm{E} \delta \mathrm{Amax}} = \underline{r}_{3} 3 \underline{I}_{(0)\mathrm{A}} (\ell_{\mathrm{A}} = \ell) \left(\frac{\underline{Z}_{\mathrm{ESlot}} (\ell_{\mathrm{A}} = \ell)}{\underline{Z}_{\mathrm{S}}^{\prime} \ell_{\mathrm{B}}} + \frac{\underline{Z}_{\mathrm{ESlot}} (\ell_{\mathrm{A}} = \ell)}{R_{\mathrm{EF}}}\right) = \underline{r}_{3} 3 \underline{I}_{(0)\mathrm{A}} (\ell_{\mathrm{A}} = \ell) \tag{54c}
$$

$$
\underline{I}_{\mathrm{E} \delta \mathrm{Bmax}} = \underline{r}_{3} 3 \underline{I}_{(0)\mathrm{B}} (\ell_{\mathrm{A}} = 0) \left(\frac{\underline{Z}_{\mathrm{ESlot}} (\ell_{\mathrm{A}} = 0)}{\underline{Z}_{\mathrm{S}}^{\prime} \ell_{\mathrm{A}}} + \frac{\underline{Z}_{\mathrm{ESlot}} (\ell_{\mathrm{A}} = 0)}{R_{\mathrm{EF}}}\right) = \underline{r}_{3} 3 \underline{I}_{(0)\mathrm{B}} (\ell \mathrm{A} = 0) \tag{55c}
$$
